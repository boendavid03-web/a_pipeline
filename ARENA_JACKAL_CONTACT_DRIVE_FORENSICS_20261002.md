## Raw force interpretation and limits

The PhysX full contact report stores the real per-point `position`, `normal`, `impulse`, `separation` and per-friction-anchor `position` and `impulse`. The analyzer projects each reported contact impulse onto its normal and sums each anchor's horizontal impulse. It computes yaw impulse around the sampled chassis XY as `(x_contact - x_chassis) * J_y - (y_contact - y_chassis) * J_x`, splitting lateral `x*J_y` from longitudinal `-y*J_x`. No `mu * normalForce` value is presented as measured friction. Contact point velocity is calculated after each step as `v_body + omega_body × (p_contact - p_body)` against the stationary ground; it cannot reveal the solver's pre-contact relative velocity. A reported point with near-zero normal impulse is not proof of load-bearing contact, and a point remaining during a spike is not proof of continuous static friction.

The contact-instrumented logs contain repeated `PxShape::getMaterialFromInternalFaceIndex ... 0xFFFFFFFF` warnings (4,098 current; 3,700 official), while older uninstrumented 230/236 logs do not. These coincide with full contact-report material lookup; they are not treated as proof that the physics material or contact solve failed. The current model reproduced the earlier 230 GroundPlane yaw and wheel-response pattern, and the final current 001/002/003 captures are identical in the measured windows. The imported model also logs 15 unresolved visual references; the official model does not. Neither run logged an error or traceback.

The local Isaac 5.1 [articulation joint sensor documentation](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/sensors/isaacsim_sensors_physics_articulation_force.html) distinguishes explicit applied effort from measured joint effort and 6D incoming joint force.
